# Game Design

The vision for the game in one place. "Decided" is settled; everything after it describes what is built and how it is tuned, and the last sections hold ideas, open questions, and what is out of scope. Implementation detail lives in the code and the tools READMEs, not here.

## Inspiration

[Zombie Estate 2](https://www.mobygames.com/game/74771/zombie-estate-ii/) is the reason this exists: a top-down zombie shooter a group of brothers played through the night. Its four pillars are what this game keeps:

- The weapons.
- The upgrades and traps between waves.
- Working out which room to survive the next round in.
- The different zombie types.

Everything else serves them. The art, names, and characters here are original. Only the shape of the fun is borrowed.

## Decided

- **The game.** A goofy-gory pixel-art wave shooter. Chunky, bright, bouncy sprites, cartoon splatter, and zombies that are funny more than scary. Every sprite moves: walk cycles, hit flashes, knockback, gibs, decals that stay, shake, and hit-stop.
- **Platform.** One game for desktop and phones, in landscape and in portrait. Every input works with a keyboard and mouse and with touch.
- **View.** Top-down pixel art on a 320×180 canvas, scaled with crisp pixels. Phones see a zoomed window that follows the player, so zombies read big under a thumb: 4/3 on their side, and 3/2 held upright on most phones (changed on 2026-10-30 at the owner's request, and zoomed back out from 15/8 on 2026-10-31 to balance portrait; see "The phone camera" and "Portrait play"). A keyboard and mouse see the whole room.
- **Controls.** Twin-stick on every device.
  - Keyboard: WASD moves. Each action has exactly one key, read by its physical position (see "One key per action"). Since 2026-10-28 the arrow keys no longer move.
  - Mouse: aim at the pointer, hold the button to fire.
  - Touch: the left thumb moves, the right thumb aims and fires while held. Two thumbs at once must work. The aim stick turns the player once it leaves its small dead zone (15% of its radius) and pulls the trigger only once pushed past 40% (`STICK_FIRE_ZONE`), so a thumb that lands fires nothing until it points somewhere (changed on 2026-10-31 after a playtester found the Barn Buster "shoots as soon as you try to aim it, so it shoots to the wrong spot": a landing thumb had no direction yet, so the shot flew where the player last faced). The mouse is unchanged.
  - Aim assist: decided with the owner on 2026-10-31 ("Yes, sideways too"). Every touch phone, upright or on its side, gets the same gentle pull of a held aim onto the zombie it is almost on; a keyboard and mouse never do. The last input decides, as it does the phone's zoom. See "Aim assist" under "Portrait play".
- **The loop.** Preparation, then a wave, then Preparation again, until the player dies or wins.
- **How the code is organized.** One Foldkit Model. A pure, deterministic `update`: no `Math.random`, no clock. Randomness comes from a seeded generator in the Model, so the same seed and the same Messages give the same game. Effect is used fully, through its own APIs, with no workarounds around it (recorded from the owner on 2026-10-29). The frame stays cheap (see "Risks").
- **Show, don't explain.** Decided with the owner on 2026-10-29. It is a fun game, so it should read at a glance and teach by doing. Prefer pictures, icons, motion, and sound to text. Keep every label and line short, cut explanations a player can learn by playing, and say a thing once: once the player has seen something (a power-up, a zombie, a control), stop repeating what it does. Older text-heavy screens are listed for the owner in `TANNER_TODO.md`.
- **Making money.** Decided with the owner on 2026-10-29. The game stays free to play.
  - **Cosmetics and a supporter pack.** One-time purchases of looks, hats, gun skins, and a founder's hat. Nothing bought changes how the game plays. What is sold and what Bones buy are separate sets (see "Rank and Bones"), so money never skips what play earns. Bought on the web through Stripe Checkout confirmed by a Worker, and kept in the player's synced scope so it follows linked devices. Not built yet.
  - **A tip jar.** A small "buy the shopkeeper a coffee" link. Built on 2026-10-29 as the TIP JAR card (a pixel mug) in the home menu's MORE hand, and a pill in the top left corner of Victory, never in a run, on pause, or on Game Over. Moved on 2026-10-30 (owner request, "make buy me a coffee on the home menu always in the top right"): the card became a coffee button that is always in the home menu's top right corner, just left of the gear (see "The home layout"); the Victory pill stays. It opens a Stripe Payment Link in a new tab and stays hidden until `SUPPORT_URL` in `src/support.ts` holds that link.
  - **Never.** No ads of any kind, no pay-to-win, and no real-money loot boxes. The mystery box, Mystery Leftovers, uses in-game money only.
- **Online is Cloudflare only.** Hosting, analytics, and player sync run on Cloudflare Workers, D1, and Durable Objects, with our own sync engine. The game lives at munchmanor.com. See `PLATFORM.md`.
- **Co-op.** Decided with the owner on 2026-10-29: online co-op for up to 4 players, server-authoritative in a Durable Object. The rules (money, revives, READY, scaling) are in "Decided" in `MULTIPLAYER.md`. Since 2026-10-30 the World plays them for 2 to 4 players offline, at one keyboard through a dev-only `?local=2` link (see "What Phase 1 built" in `MULTIPLAYER.md`); online play is not built yet. In co-op each player may hold one Plank Pile and one Grumpy Gnome, the shop and the break never pause the world, and a downed player is revived by a teammate standing still beside them.
- **Original art and names.** Nothing is copied from Zombie Estate 2: no art, names, or item lists.
- **Names.** Decided with the owner on 2026-10-30, after a playtester found that the upgrade names hid that each was the same gun. Each gun has one base name: the Pop Pistol, the Barn Buster, and the Chatterbox (the SMG; the owner picked Chatterbox on 2026-10-30). Its upgrade tiers are the base name plus one tier word from a ladder every gun shares: tier 1 is Beefy (Beefy Barn Buster), tier 2 is DELUXE (Barn Buster DELUXE), and tier 3 is 3000 (Barn Buster 3000). The ladder is `TIER_LADDER` in `src/names.ts`, and a new tier is a new word on the ladder, never a new name for one gun. Traps and power-ups have no tiers, so their names stay as they are. Every name a player reads (guns, zombies, traps, power-ups, shop goods) lives in that one file, keyed by id, so a rename never touches an id or a save.
- **The knife and two gun slots.** Decided with the owner on 2026-10-30. Every player always carries a knife: a quick, weak stab right in front, about 0.35 s apart, with a small shove, that never runs out. Changed on 2026-10-30 at the owner's request ("On the right of the guns", "Should be equipped on tap, just like the guns"): the knife is a third slot right of the two gun slots, drawn like a gun (a tap on its slot, or F), and then fire stabs; Q or a tap on a gun slot goes back to a gun. Before, F or a round knife button left of the slots was held to stab beside the gun. It is a simple tool with no upgrades; the only melee upgrade is the Clang Pan from Mystery Leftovers. Changed on 2026-10-30 at the owner's request ("The frying pan should replace the knife, not a gun slot"): taking the Pan puts it in the melee slot in place of the knife for the rest of the run, both guns stay, and a new run starts with the knife again; before, the Pan took a gun slot like the Cooker and Brain Freeze. The player has two gun slots, and the Pop Pistol starts in one but is no longer locked: buying a gun (or taking the Cooker or Brain Freeze from the fridge) with both slots full replaces the gun the player picks, so the pistol can be traded away for two real guns. Without the pistol's endless ammo a player can run dry, which is what the knife and Rattles' ammo are for. Rattles always sells the Pop Pistol back for $0. Built on 2026-10-30: see "The knife and two gun slots" below.
- **Stand still or hold to buy.** Decided with the owner on 2026-10-30 ("make it so if you just stand next to these in round purchases for the time allotment you purchase it / swap it. Just like how healing works!"), replacing hold-only. Every purchase in the world (a boarded exit, a fridge pull, taking a fridge prize) is bought by standing still within reach for 1 s, the way a teammate revives, or by holding the interact key (or the touch hold button) for 0.5 s, as before. Both fill the same ring, and the key fills it twice as fast. A second reads as a choice, not a pause between strafes, and is half a revive, since walking away undoes a buy. Guarded against accidents: standing counts only while the player neither moves, fires, nor stabs, never while the shop is open or in the first 1.5 s of a wave, and never short of the price (no red shake; only a key press is refused). After any buy the player must move or press the key before standing buys again, so one buy never chains into the next: a fridge pull by standing does not take its prize until the player steps. Leaving a prize is unchanged: walk away or let the time run out. World state stepped from each seat's intent, so in co-op each player buys alone (protocol version 9). Checked with `src/standToBuy.story.test.ts`. One exception, asked for by the owner on 2026-10-31 ("make it so you can tap to buy ammo mid round instead of waiting for the timer"): at the ammo window (below) a tap buys at once, with no ring. A press of Enter, a tap of the touch hold button, or a click or tap on the ammo tag buys one refill on the next tick, or shakes the tag red when the wallet is short or the gun is full; holding Enter on buys nothing more until the key is let go and pressed again. Standing still there still fills the ring for a second, so walking past never buys. The boards and the fridge keep the hold: they cost far more, and a hold makes them a choice. The tap is the seat action `BuyAmmo`, so in co-op each player taps for themselves (protocol version 14). Checked with `src/ammoTap.story.test.ts`. The balance bot still holds the key; with standing on, 100 Normal campaign runs won 11% (11% of 100 before), bought the kitchen in 97% and pulled the fridge in 79% of runs, and took 100 of the 101 weapons offered, so standing and shooting near a door buys nothing by accident.
- **The ammo window.** Decided with the owner on 2026-10-30, after a friend's first death came from running the Chatterbox dry with no way to fix it. Mid-wave the stall's CLOSED sign becomes a small AMMO window. A tap there (Enter, the hold button, or the tag; 2026-10-31), or standing still at it (the rule above), refills the gun in the player's hands at the shop's ammo price: a Shell Sack for the Barn Buster, a Chatter Drum for the Chatterbox, a Lid Stack for the Pressure Cooker. The tag beside the stall shows a box of rounds and the price, or FULL for a full gun (a press shakes it, standing never buys). A gun with nothing to refill (the Pop Pistol, the Clang Pan, Brain Freeze) gets no offer. It never sells guns, traps, upgrades, hearts, or repairs mid-wave; the full shop still opens only in breaks. Rattles dozes behind the window until a player steps up, then wakes; a sale slides a box of rounds across his counter and he says a line (FRESH ROUNDS!, NO REFUNDS, and so on). In co-op each player buys for themselves (protocol version 10). Built in `src/ammoCounter.ts`, checked with `src/ammoCounter.story.test.ts`. The balance bot walks to the window mid-wave when the gun in its hands runs low, it stands within 70 px of the stall, and it has the price, and holds the key there (`AMMO=0` is the bot that never does). Measured 2026-10-30 (Normal campaign, 100 runs each, the same code): 13% wins with the window (47 refills, 0.5 a run) against 11% without it, within a few points, so no mid-wave markup.
- **The Barn Buster hits hard up close.** Changed on 2026-10-31 after a playtester found the shotgun "too weak": point blank it did less than the Pop Pistol (8 damage against 11 in two seconds of held fire on a Groaner 24 px away), since its wide spray and big shove sent most pellets past. It now fires 7 pellets (was 5) in a 0.55 radian spray (was 0.75), every 580 ms (was 620) with a 1.2 s reload (was 1.3), a shove of 180 (was 200), and a reach of about 100 px (was 83). Up close it now out-hits the pistol by half (`src/shop.story.test.ts`, "the Barn Buster up close"). It stays a short-range gun, cheaper than the Chatterbox and behind it at range: the balance model's sustained damage is 5.38 a second against the Chatterbox's 5.45. Measured 2026-10-31 (Normal campaign, 100 runs): wave 2, the shotgun's wave, lost 0.04 hearts against 0.31 in the baseline, and wins were 13% against 16%, within noise, since the bot trades it for the Chatterbox at the next break.
- **Rank and Bones.** Decided with the owner on 2026-10-31 ("I like the idea of an account level/rank!"). Every run, lost or won, fills a rank bar and pays Bones, a currency kept for good. Rank unlocks callings (a small trade-off picked before a run) and new guns, traps, and fridge prizes as they are built; Bones buy earned cosmetics in the menus. Neither ever makes a player stronger: no stat boosts between runs. Rank shows beside each name in the co-op lobby and on the crew board, and nowhere in a run. Phase 1 (points, Bones, the badge, the end card's strip) is built; see "Rank and Bones" below.
- **Working title.** Munch Manor, in `GAME_TITLE` in `src/constants.ts`. `index.html` repeats it for the first paint, and a Scene test checks the two agree.

### Exploring the manor

Decided with the owner on 2026-10-29. This replaces the first playable's one-room arena and its "5 to 10 minute run" as the shape of the campaign.

- **Buy rooms open.** One manor per run. Every run starts in the parlour, whose exits are boarded. The player buys an exit open with money, so every dollar is a choice between a gun and a room. An opened room adds space, loot, and options, and its doors join the spawn doors.
- **The campaign grows.** The campaign itself becomes the manor run, so a run gets longer than the first playable's 5 to 10 minutes. There is no separate quick mode. The wave count and run length are set as rooms land, with the balance tools.
  - **Ten waves, proposed with the kitchen on 2026-10-29, for the owner to review.** Waves 1 to 7 are as they were. Wave 8 is a breather after the Ankle Biters: a long build of mixed squads and pincers with one Big Lug. Wave 9 tests everything before the finale: Ankle Biters, a Big Leaguer, two Big Lugs, a Hotfoot rush, and a closing pincer. Wave 10 is the old wave 8 finale with Uncle Chunk, who now comes every tenth wave, so endless meets him on waves 20, 30, and on. The kitchen can be bought from the wave 3 break; the waves assume it may be open, through their open door groups, and stay winnable when it is never opened (the `KITCHEN=0` bot). See "The kitchen".
- **Rooms cost money.** Proposed with the kitchen on 2026-10-29, for the owner to review: the kitchen costs $60. See "The kitchen".
- **Buy rooms any time.** Changed by the owner on 2026-10-30 (it was "explore in breaks"): a room can be bought open any time the round runs, mid-wave too, and its doors join the current wave right away. It is bought by standing still or holding the interact key at the boards, so nothing pops up and the world keeps going. The wave keeps its zombies, kinds, and times: only doors change, so the sawtooth, the pacing rules, and the balance tools stay valid. See "Its doors in the waves" under "The kitchen".
- **The kitchen first.** The first room after the parlour is the kitchen: ovens to trip and slippery soup. The level is already data in the Model, so a room is an exit in a `LevelSpec` (see "Technical notes").
- **No off-screen hints.** Nothing on screen ever points at zombies. Door preview arrows may point at doors outside the view during breaks only, never during a wave (owner decision 2026-10-29). In a wave the door rattles and groans carry the warning.
  - **Teammates are the exception.** Decided on 2026-10-29 with co-op: an off-screen teammate shows as a small avatar at the screen edge. Zombies and doors still get nothing.
- **Still a sketch.** Rooms with one signature twist each: library (bookshelf passages, a rare-gun stash), ballroom (open floor for hordes, a chandelier to shoot down), cellar (dark, lit only near the player), a graveyard outside (floor spawns, Uncle Chunk's arena), attic (one entrance, a risky stash). A goal beyond surviving, such as restoring the power or finding the keys to Uncle Chunk's cellar. Interactive pieces such as a fireplace trap or a dumbwaiter. The mystery box is named: **Mystery Leftovers**, a grimy fridge in the kitchen; pay in-game money, open it, and grab whatever gun is growing inside (decided with the owner on 2026-10-30). Its prizes, decided the same day, are fridge-only weapons that Rattles never sells: the **Pressure Cooker** (hold to build pressure, release to launch a lid that explodes; more charge, bigger blast; holding past full pops it in your hands; 4 lids a load, any charge costs one), the **Clang Pan** (melee with huge knockback that dazes small zombies and home-runs thrown ones; no ammo, a dent meter slows it until Rattles bangs it flat for free each break), and **Brain Freeze** (freezes small zombies so any hit shatters them; charges only, no refills). Once a player has one, Rattles sells its upgrades on the shared tier ladder and its ammo (a Lid Stack for the Cooker). Each pull costs about $75 of in-game money, works through the same hold-to-buy prompt as the doors, and draws from its own seeded stream so it never changes which zombies come. Built on 2026-10-30: see "Mystery Leftovers" below. The layout stays hand-made, while secrets and loot spots shift with the seed.

### The first playable

Decided on 2026-10-27: a fun one-room arena a stranger can open from a link. It is built and live. Where a line below was later changed, the change is dated.

- **Audience.** Strangers on the web. The first 30 seconds have to hook someone who knows nothing about the game.
- **Run length.** Superseded on 2026-10-29 by "Exploring the manor": the campaign grows with the manor. Today's eight-wave campaign takes about 8 minutes for the bot.
- **The arena.** The parlour: one 320×180 room with cover and four doorways. Since 2026-10-29 portrait phones see it through a window that follows the player, and since 2026-10-30 phones on their side do too.
- **Waves.** 10 waves since the kitchen (proposed), the last a Tank-heavy horde with Uncle Chunk on top, then Victory. The count grows with the manor (see "Exploring the manor"). Endless keeps going past the campaign.
- **Preparation.** About 20 seconds, timed and skippable. Since 2026-10-28 the first break, before wave 2, has no timer and waits for START WAVE (Enter).
- **Shop.** A stall in the room, opened by walking into it.
- **Weapons.** A pistol that never runs out, plus one bought gun with limited ammo. Two carried, swapped with one key.
- **Zombies.** Six kinds and a boss (see "Zombie types"). The Ankle Biter comes up through the floor, which bends "doors are the chokepoints" on purpose: it stays rare, telegraphs for 1.5 s, never comes up near the player, and the preview counts it as FLOOR.
- **Traps.** The Plank Pile and the Grumpy Gnome. At most two Plank Piles, placed or carried (2026-10-28). Groaners and Big Lugs chew a nearby pile instead of chasing the player (2026-10-28). A boarded doorway is no spawn point (2026-10-29).
- **Difficulty levels.** Decided on 2026-10-28: Easy, Normal (the default), Hard, and Nightmare, locked until a Hard campaign win. See "Difficulty levels and planks as bait".
- **Health.** A few hearts, at most 5. A hit knocks the player back and gives a short invulnerable blink. Hearts are bought and rarely dropped.
- **Onboarding.** Wave 1 is the tutorial: a few slow Groaners and control hints for the current device. No walls of text.
- **Sound.** Sound effects unlocked on the first input, and synthesized chiptune music that follows the run.

#### Onboarding

Built on 2026-10-28 after a first-time phone player said the right stick was never taught and new zombies came in unexplained. The logic is in `src/onboarding.ts`.

- **Hints are pictures.** Decided with the owner on 2026-10-29. Each hint is a small moving pixel icon for the current device plus at most three words: WASD keys pressing or a thumb on a stick for MOVE, a clicking mouse or a firing stick for AIM + SHOOT, and once a trap is bought the T key or a tapping hand before planks on a door (BLOCK DOOR) or a gnome (PLONK IT). The icons are in `src/hintIcons.ts`.
- **Teach where the player acts.** Changed on 2026-10-30 after two playtesters read the break's floating labels (GO SHOP, BLOCK and SHOOT) as places to walk to, or as a button that builds traps, and the owner agreed they made little sense. Only the control hints above float. The rest is taught at the thing itself: in a break Rattles calls from the stall with a speech bubble of a coin, a Plank Pile, and a gnome until the first trap is bought (pinned to the window's edge with an arrow when the stall is out of view, and a lone coin there while the shop has never been opened); the shop's TRAPS tab wears NEW and its traps pulse until then; the place trap button pulses until the first one is placed; START WAVE pulses until the first wave is started; the gun not held pulses until the first swap; a goodie wears a bouncing arrow until the first grab; and the first timed break's countdown blinks red. The bubble is `src/stallBubble.ts`.
- **The guided first break.** Built on 2026-10-30 from a playtester who wanted the game to walk them to the shop after the first wave, as other games do. In a new save's first shop break, one step at a time, each taught at its spot, with a small tracker (three icons, the current one lit with GO, BUY, or PLACE, and a skip button) that never covers what it teaches: in landscape it docks in the top corner away from the step's spot, in portrait it sits compact in the band above the world: walk to Rattles (his stall waves and calls with a bubble of a coin and the traps, pinned to the window's edge when out of view; outside the tour that bubble shows only while the stall is in view, and never during the kitchen tour), buy a Plank Pile (the TRAPS tab wears NEW, the traps pulse, and the first Plank Pile is FREE, so the lesson never dead-ends on an empty wallet), and place it (an arrow bobs over the nearest open doorway, the place button pulses, and the T or tap hint shows). Placed, every step ticks, confetti pops, the tracker fades, and START WAVE pulses. It starts only while the shop lesson is pending and no trap was ever bought, so it runs once per save, a player who has shopped never sees it, and a tutorial reset plays it again. Never in co-op, where a room's lessons are learned and planks cost full price. Skipping it, or starting the wave, ends it. The free Plank Pile is `isFirstPlankFree` in `src/shop.ts`, read by `priceFor`; the steps are `src/firstBreakQuest.ts`, the tracker `src/questView.ts`. The balance bot and the determinism script play a save past the tutorial.
- **The kitchen tour.** Chapter 2 of the guided tutorial, from the owner on 2026-10-30 ("a free purchase for the kitchen after wave 3, and free mystery box roll then too ... pause the timer"). In a new save's first timed break, the one where the kitchen's boards first go on sale, the break clock holds (the tracker shows an hourglass with a pause mark, and the countdown stops blinking), the boards' tag says FREE with a gift bubble over them (pinned to the window's edge when out of view), then the fridge's first pull is FREE with the bubble over it, and the pull is always a weapon: the same draw from the fridge's own stream against the weapons' weights alone, so it never shifts the waves or the drops. While the prize floats, its TAKE tag and filling ring teach taking, and a small bubble under the tag, footprints and a cross, teaches leaving it by walking away. The clock runs again once the fridge has been tried: the prize taken, left, or sunk back (a Sandwich or a Dud counts too). It runs once per save, since it is on only while the timed breaks lesson is pending, and a tutorial reset brings it back. Never in co-op, where the shared clock never holds and prices stay. The tracker's skip button learns the lesson at once. The rules that change the game are `src/tutorialRules.ts` (the prices go through the offers' `price` in `src/purchase.ts`), the steps `src/kitchenTour.ts`.
- **Hints clear on practice.** The move hint clears after 24 px of the player's own movement, the aim and fire hint after 3 aimed shots, neither before 2.5 s of play. Other input leaves them alone.
- **Touch teaches both thumbs.** During wave 1, pulsing ghost sticks sit on each half of the screen until that thumb's lesson is learned.
- **The practice gate.** Wave 1's countdown waits until the player has moved and aimed and fired. START WAVE (Enter) skips it. A returning player never sees it.
- **The new zombie card.** The moment a wave starts, the first kind in it not yet introduced gets a band across the screen: its walk cycle large, its name, and a one-line pitch. The world holds still for 5.5 s; after 1.5 s a tap, a click, or Enter skips it. Each kind is introduced once, saved on the device. During Preparation the door preview already tags the door with NEW, so the card is never the first warning.

| Kind        | Card pitch                                             |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Groaner     | HE'S SLOW. BACK UP AND KEEP SHOOTING.                  |
| Hotfoot     | ZOOMS RIGHT AT YOU. ONE SHOT DROPS IT.                 |
| Kaboomer    | HE WANTS A HUG. POP HIM FROM FAR AWAY.                 |

- **The door preview.** On the overlay canvas, which never shakes. Each door's box lists the kinds coming through it with an icon and a count; only its frame pulses.

#### Screen shake

Retuned on 2026-10-28 after a playtest said shooting shook too much. Trauma moves the screen up to 3 px per unit and decays 2.5 units a second. Shots can raise it only to 0.3, so held fire shakes about 1 px at most; hurts, blasts, big kills, and the boss add on top, up to 1. The numbers per source are in `src/juice.ts` and `src/constants.ts`. Screen shake can be turned off in SETTINGS.

#### Music

Original, synthesized with Web Audio, no audio files: a goofy, swung haunted-house groove in E minor. The patterns are data in `src/music.ts`.

- **Moods.** Creep (100 BPM) on the title and in Preparation, Romp (138 BPM) in a wave, Stampede (152 BPM) on the finale and endless. Stingers for a cleared wave, Victory, and Game Over.
- **Who owns what.** `update` decides only the mood and any stinger and sends `CueMusic` when it changes. The scheduler lives with the audio resource. Music never feeds back into the Model. M mutes everything, N toggles music.

#### Proposed balance

Starting numbers to play against, not decisions. They live in `src/constants.ts`, `src/weapons.ts`, `src/zombies.ts`, `src/waves.ts`, and `src/shop.ts`.

- **Money.** Each kill pays its kind's bounty. Clearing wave _n_ pays $20 + $5 × _n_. Wave 1 pays about $37, so the first visit buys a gun.
- **The shop** opens from the wave 2 Preparation. Before wave 1 the stall sleeps behind a CLOSED sign; mid-wave it is the ammo window, which sells only the held gun's ammo (see "The ammo window").
- **Slots.** The pistol always stays in the first slot. A bought gun replaces whatever is in the second, with its ammo.

| Item                  | Price | What it does                                                                                                      |
| --------------------- | ----- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pop Pistol            | Start | 1 damage, 150 ms between shots, a 14 round magazine, 0.8 s reload, never runs out                                 |
| Barn Buster (shotgun) | $35   | 7 pellets in a 0.55 radian spray, 1 damage each, short range, 580 ms between shots, 6 shells a load, 1.2 s reload |
| Shell Sack            | $10   | 16 more shells, up to 48. The shotgun comes with 16                                                               |
| Chatterbox (SMG)      | $100  | 1 damage, 70 ms between shots, a slight spread, a 30 round magazine, 1.2 s reload                                 |
| Chatter Drum          | $20   | 120 more rounds, up to 360. The SMG comes with 150                                                                |
| Pickled Heart         | $25   | One heart, never past 5                                                                                           |

- **Reloading.** Every gun fires from a magazine. An empty one reloads on its own; R, or a tap on the held gun's slot, reloads early. On Easy and Normal a gun with room in its magazine also reloads on its own once the trigger has rested 0.7 s with it in hand (the knife or Pan out, the counter, and a fresh swap all start the wait over); Hard and Nightmare leave that to R. Swapping cancels a reload, and each gun keeps its own magazine. The pistol fires 6.7 shots a second while a magazine lasts and 4.75 sustained.
- **Out of ammo.** Changed on 2026-10-30 after a playtester traded up to the Chatterbox and died not knowing it could run dry. The gun slot shows the Pop Pistol's rounds as a count over ∞ and any other gun's as a count over what is left. From about two magazines left the count blinks amber, and on the last magazine red with the slot's border, and each of the last five shots plays a low tick. #### Packs and pacing

Proposed, not decided. The packs are in `src/waves.ts`, the beats, telegraphs, and intensity in `src/pacing.ts`.

- **Packs.** Zombies come in packs, not a drizzle: a Squad from one door, a Pincer from opposite doors 1.5 to 3 s apart, a Horde from every door, a Trickle of one.
- **Sawtooth.** A wave runs two or three cycles of Build, Peak (5 to 8 s), Fade, and Relax. At a hold the wave clock stops until fewer than 6 zombies are alive and nothing waits at a door, then rests 2 to 3 s. A fade gives up after 20 s. The last peak is the biggest.
- **Tighter waves.** From owner feedback on 2026-10-29 ("Waves shouldn't be super long or have periods where no zombies are on screen"). After 0.5 s of quiet (no zombie alive, nothing at a door) the clock skips to the next pack's warning. A relax that goes quiet lets in up to two stragglers, then ends. The restless extension is at most 2 s. Endless relaxes are 3 s, falling 0.1 s a wave to 2 s.
- **Telegraphs.** Every pack warns about 1 s before it enters: its door rattles with red eyes, dust puffs, and a groan plays. A horde rings the DINNER BELL instead. The pack is in its doorway a beat after the warning ends, about 1.2 s in all for every kind and door: outside the room, where nothing is drawn, a zombie walks at least 50 px/s. A warned pack keeps its time while the wave clock stops for a fade, a relax, or the boss, and a pack the alive cap has no place for waits to warn instead of rattling its door.
- **Doorway camping.** A pack due while the player stands within 16 px of its doorway waits until the player steps away. Only timing changes, never a door or a kind, so the preview stays exact.
- **The alive cap.** At most 20 zombies in the room in every mode; the rest wait to warn until a place frees up, and a warned pack's stragglers wait at its door. It is the phone's frame budget.

#### Mercy rules

Proposed, not decided. Invisible and deterministic; neither draws from the seeded generator.

- **Last-heart grace.** A hit that leaves 1 heart blinks for 1.5 s instead of 1 s.
- **Pacing intensity.** `intensity` rises with hearts lost and zombies close by and decays about 11% a second. While it is at least 0.4 when a relax ends, the relax runs up to 2 s longer. It never adds or removes a zombie.

#### Difficulty principles

These guide tuning. The code does not enforce them.

- **Stay in the flow channel.** Challenge rises about as fast as skill and gear. Each wave teaches, tests, combines, or twists.
- **Sawtooth intensity.** During a peak, incoming zombie health a second briefly reaches 1.5 to 2.5 times the player's damage a second; during a relax it is near zero.
- **Telegraph every threat.** Surprise comes from what a warning means, never from a zombie with no warning.
- **Invisible mercy.** Small hidden help at the edge of death makes close calls more common than deaths.
- **No rubber banding.** What comes, and from where, is fixed by the seed when Preparation begins.
- **Measure with the bot, then playtest.** The targets are for the bot, not for people. Since 2026-10-31 the default bot plays like a person (late reactions, trailing aim, a narrow eye) for Easy and Normal, and the old sharp bot (`AIM=sharp`) stands in for a skilled player on Hard and Nightmare.

#### How balance is checked

Added on 2026-10-29. Three layers, cheapest first. The commands are in `AGENTS.md` and `tools/balance/README.md`.

- **The analytical model** (`tools/balance/model.ts`, instant, part of `bun run test`). From the real tables, for every wave and level: the spawns for six seeds, the money the waves pay, the loadout the bot's shopping list buys, that loadout's sustained damage a second, and the wave played as a flow of health through the holds, rests, quiet hurries, trickles, and the alive cap. Its tests hold the guardrails: waves 5 to 8 peak at 1.5 to 2.5 times the player's damage, early waves are gentler, health never falls from one wave to the next on any level, the peak never drops more than 0.05 from one wave to the next, no wave runs past its length ceiling, and Easy < Normal < Hard < Nightmare. A guardrail not met yet is a `test.todo` with today's value; since 2026-10-29 every one is met. The model ranks changes and spots gross mistakes; the bot has the last word.
- **Wave snapshots** (`bun run balance -- --wave 4 --runs 200`, seconds). The bot plays one wave many times from typical run states taken from ten campaign runs that reached it. It reports cleared, hearts lost, lengths, empty and quiet seconds, the longest quiet stretch, and deaths by kind. A three-run smoke pass of wave 2 runs in `bun run test`.
- **The nightly suite** (`bun run balance:nightly`, minutes). Every campaign bot on every level, endless, portrait passes (Normal and Easy with `VIEW=portrait`), and every wave's snapshots, held to `tools/balance/targets.ts` and to `tools/balance/baseline.json`. Its report lists what is in target, what drifted past its noise, and what is out of target. A portrait pass more than 8 points from its landscape twin is called out.

**The targets** (in `tools/balance/targets.ts`):

- **Wins.** Normal: the human bot 20 to 35%, `SWAP=1` 30 to 45% (kept: the human bot with `SWAP=1` won 42% on 2026-10-31). Easy 60 to 75% on the human bot. Hard about 5 to 12% and Nightmare about 1 to 4% on `AIM=sharp`. Portrait is held to the same targets. Why these (2026-10-31): the human bot on the old tables lost about as often as real players did (Easy 1%, Normal 0%, against 7% and 4% in production), so Easy should be won by most people who finish a run and Normal by a solid quarter to a third of them, with Hard a real step up for players who aim well.
- **Where runs die.** Under 3% in waves 1 and 2, under 10% in 3 and 4, about 15 to 30% in 5 and 6, and the most in 7 to 10, with the finale the biggest single wall for the runs that reach it. Restated on 2026-10-31 for the human bot, which dies over the whole back half instead of in one band (it was 25 to 35% in 5 and 6 and the most on the finale): on Normal it dies on waves 6, 7, 9, and 10 in 16, 21, 10, and 14% of runs, and the finale kills 35% of the runs that reach it against wave 7's 32%.
- **Feel.** A median win of 9 to 12 minutes for today's campaign. 40 to 65% of wins on Normal are close calls (down to 1 heart), restated on 2026-10-31 from 30 to 50%: the human bot takes bites it never saw coming, so its wins scrape by more often (62%) than the sharp bot's did (41%).
- **Wave length.** The median wave in the snapshot pass: wave 1 at most 40 s, waves 2 to 4 at most 75 s, waves 5 to 7 at most 90 s, wave 8 with the boss at most 120 s.
- **Dead air.** At most 3 quiet seconds a wave on the mean run and no stretch over 1.2 s in any run. A Story test holds a perfect killer's longest quiet stretch to 1 s.

Before that (nightly, 2026-10-30, the sharp bot, 200 runs a row, after the balance pass: the portrait aim assist, an easier Hard and Nightmare, the Chatter Drum at $20, and rooms bought mid-wave): Easy 56.5%, Normal 16% (`SWAP=1` 46.5%, close calls 41%, median win 10.2 min), Hard 4.5%, Nightmare 0.5%, portrait Normal 14.5% and Easy 54%. 41 of 47 targets in target; the six misses are each within about 2.5 points: `SWAP=1`, Hard, Nightmare, both portrait passes, and Normal deaths in waves 5 and 6 (22.5%). **The bot** (`tools/balance/simulate.ts`, always through `bun run balance`). ### Portrait play

#### Uncle Chunk

Reworked on 2026-10-28 from owner feedback: "Should feel like a challenge, not something you can just run around." The rules are `BOSS_RULES` in `src/boss.ts`.

- **Who.** A hulking pot-bellied zombie with a tiny head and a tiny green party hat, about twice a Big Lug each way.
- **Numbers.** Health 90 times the wave's health scale, the level's health, and the level's own boss health (on wave 10: 54 on Easy, 68 on Normal, 146 on Hard, 167 on Nightmare). His belched Groaners take the wave's and the level's health, not his boss health. Slow, but 1.6 times as fast while the player is more than 70 px away. A bite costs 2 hearts. Bounty $150, and his kill always drops a goodie.
- **Range decides the attack.** Within 44 px he stomps; beyond 70 px he charges and spits in turn; between, he walks in and belches. Standing near him and stepping out of the stomp is the safe way to fight him.
- **Stomp.** A 34 px red ring fills around him for 1 s, then costs 1 heart and shoves.
- **Charge.** A red lane fills toward where the player stood, then he rushes along it for 2 hearts. A wall, a crate, or a barricade stops him with a bonk, and he is dazed and takes double damage for 1.8 s.
- **Spit.** A glob arcs to a marked spot for 1 heart and leaves a slowing puddle.
- **Belch.** Every 15 s or so he burps up a Groaner, never past the alive cap.
- **Enraged.** At a third of his health he roars, flushes red, moves 1.5 times as fast, and waits less between attacks. His tells stay the same length.
- **One tell at a time.** Every attack winds up on the floor under the actors, so nothing hides it.
- **When he comes.** The finale's preview shows a BOSS marker at his door. He walks in once two thirds of the wave has come, the clock holds for 5 s, and then the rest of the wave joins him. His bar sits at the top of the screen. Victory waits until he has fallen and every other zombie is dead.
- **The Tank-heavy finale holds.** Wave 8 still ends with the most Big Lugs of any wave and a horde, with the boss on top. On 2026-10-29 the tighter pacing trimmed that to three Big Lugs and a one-volley horde, a balance number, not a change to the decision.

#### Boss balance

The bot dodges every tell and shoots him unless another zombie is within 60 px. Kept on 2026-10-28 after 21 tries: health 90, with a median kill of 40 s in winning runs; most of what he costs is the time the horde has while he stands. The finale's health scale went from ×1.21 to ×1.1 so the boss is the step up. `KITE=1` (running away) wins about 1%, as intended.

**Tougher on Hard and Nightmare, 2026-10-31.** The owner: "the boss has wayyyy too little HP on hard. Should make it better." On Hard he had 97 health (90 × the level's ×1.08; the finale's wave scale is ×1), so a late loadout melted him: the Chatterbox 3000 alone fires about 34 damage a second, so a skilled player who focuses him drops him in a few seconds. Each level now has its own boss health (`bossHealthScale` in `src/difficulty.ts`), on top of the level's health, so he becomes the finale without a tougher crowd: Easy and Normal ×1 (as the easing left them, which already lowers him to 54 and 68), Hard ×1.5 (146), Nightmare ×1.6 (167, at least as tough as Hard). Co-op's crowd scale (×1.1 to ×1.3 by player count) still multiplies on top. Endless brings him back every tenth wave with the same scales.

### Rank and Bones

Decided with the owner on 2026-10-31, from a brainstorm of how other games keep players coming back: Zombie Estate 2's points that unlock for good, Kongregate's badges and ranks, Vampire Survivors' and Brotato's unlocks that widen a run, and Hades' rewards for every run. The owner chose a rank and a currency, rewards of looks, new content, and callings, no power between runs, and rank in the co-op lobby. The numbers below are a first pass, to tune with the balance bot and a playtest.

- **Rank points.** A run pays 10 points per wave cleared, 50 for felling Uncle Chunk, and 100 for a campaign win, times the difficulty: Easy ×0.75, Normal ×1, Hard ×1.5, Nightmare ×2. Endless pays its waves the same way. In co-op every player gets the crew's points, plus 10 for each crew board award and 5 for each revive. The tutorial and practice pay nothing. A Normal run that dies on wave 7 pays 60; a Normal win pays 250.
- **Ranks.** Rank 1 to 2 takes 100 points, and each rank after takes 25 more than the last (rank 10 at about 1,800 points, about 8 Normal wins). There is no top rank. The badge changes look every 10 ranks (wood, tin, bronze, silver, gold, then a goofy one), so a number and a picture say how long someone has played.
- **Bones.** A run pays one Bone per 10 rank points, rounded down: 6 for that wave 7 loss, 25 for a Normal win. Bones never buy anything in a run and never buy power.
- **What rank unlocks.** At set ranks, the same for everyone, in a fixed order:
  - **Callings** at ranks 3, 6, 10, and 15. A calling is picked before a run (YOU, the default, has none) and shown by a prop on the player's own look, so the Look tab still decides the face. Each is a trade-off, never a straight boost. First four, all original: the **Handyman** (Plank Piles cost half, one fewer heart), the **Track Star** (runs 10% faster, magazines 20% smaller), the **Penny Pincher** (Rattles charges 15% less, kills pay 15% less), and the **Line Cook** (starts with the Pressure Cooker in place of the Pop Pistol, cannot buy the pistol back).   - **Cosmetic gifts** at the ranks between: a hat, an outfit color, or a dice face, free.
- **What Bones buy.** Cosmetics only, at a closet in the Profile beside the Look tab: hats about 40 to 80 Bones, outfit colors and prints about 20, gun skins about 60. Earned cosmetics and the supporter pack's are separate sets.

The kind id stays `Thrower`. A lanky mint pitcher in a torn pinstripe jersey and a backwards cap, knuckles dragging.

### Difficulty levels and planks as bait

From owner feedback on 2026-10-28, all approved. Built, not yet playtested. The code is in `src/difficulty.ts`, `src/doorRoute.ts`, `maybeBaitDoor` in `src/simulation.ts`, and `BAIT_RULES` in `src/traps.ts`.

#### The plank cap

At most two Plank Piles, counting placed and carried. A third is refused with a short reason. With planks cheap, boarding every door was the answer to everything; two still close the two busiest doors.

And at most half the room's open doors take planks, whoever placed them (owner, 2026-10-31: a co-op crew of four could board up the whole parlour). That is 2 of the parlour's 4 doors and 3 of 6 with the kitchen open; a plank past it is refused with "Half the doors are boarded". Solo's cap of two never reaches it. The rule is `isHalfBoarded` in `src/traps.ts`.

#### Boarded doors are not spawn points

From owner feedback on 2026-10-29 ("zombies are spawning behind the windows that are boarded up").

- **The rule.** A doorway with a standing Plank Pile is no spawn point. Its packs come through the next open door clockwise. A pincer's two packs keep to two different open doors.
- **Live and honest.** The preview, the rattle, camping, and the arrival all read the rerouted door, so placing a plank moves the preview at once. Once a pack has rattled, its door is kept.
- **Not rerouted.** Ankle Biters already avoid doorways. Uncle Chunk keeps his door and smashes through.

#### Slow zombies chew planks

- **Who.** Groaners and Big Lugs, the slow walkers. Everything else, frenzied packs, and the boss ignore planks.
- **The rule.** A slow zombie in the room goes for the nearest standing pile when its walk there is at most 60 px and shorter than its walk to the player, and chews it from inside. Each doorway's field is built once at load, so a frame costs two array lookups per slow zombie per plank.
- **Bait, not a win.** A plank buys a few seconds per zombie and costs the plank.

Fewer runs die early (Hard waves 5 and 6: 35.5% to 24%; Nightmare waves 3 and 4: 35.5% to 2%), and more now reach the later walls.

- **The first pick.** The first PLAY of a session asks HOW TOUGH ARE YOU? with the four levels as big buttons. After that the badge and SETTINGS change it.
- **Nightmare.** Shown locked until a Hard campaign win, which unlocks it with a fanfare and a NEW! badge.
- **Shown.** Game Over and Victory show the difficulty first, and the bests are kept per level.

## Core loop

| Phase         | What the player does                                                                      |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Ready`       | The home menu.                                                                            |
| `Preparation` | Walks the manor, spends money on guns, traps, and rooms, and picks where to make a stand. |
| `Wave`        | Survives. Zombies come through the doors and up through the floor.                        |
| `GameOver`    | Sees the run's stats and goes again. `Victory` follows the campaign's last wave.          |

The phase is a tagged union, never a set of booleans.

- **The map.** A grid of tiles: floors, walls, doors, and boarded exits. Zombies find the player through a flow field toward them. A small room is easy to defend and easy to get cornered in; a big one has more space and more entrances.
- **The preview.** Before each wave the game shows which zombies are coming and through which doors. Without it the choice of room would be a guess.
- **Weapons are data.** Fire rate, damage, spread, ammo, reload, and tiers are table rows, so a new gun is a data change.
- **Shop dialogs** are ordinary Foldkit views over the canvas: large touch targets, keyboard, and screen readers.

## Zombie types

A kind is a row in an exhaustive table (stats, a behavior tag, how it dies, its sounds) with its sprites in a second table keyed the same way, so a new kind will not compile until it has both. Bursts go through one function that takes any targets. Wide walkers (the Big Lug and Uncle Chunk) follow their own flow field that only steps where a 2×2 block is open.

| Type     | Name shown to players | Idea                                                                             |
| -------- | --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Shambler | Groaner               | The baseline. Slow, average health.                                              |
| Runner   | Hotfoot               | Fast and fragile. Punishes a room with many doors.                               |
| Tank     | Big Lug               | Slow and very tough. Rewards traps and strong guns.                              |
| Exploder | Kaboomer              | Hugs a bomb that goes off when he dies, hurting nearby traps and players.        |
| Thrower  | Big Leaguer           | Hangs back, lobs Groaners at the player, and shields himself with a held zombie. |
| Crawler  | Ankle Biter           | Comes up through the floor and grabs the ankles.                                 |
| Boss     | Uncle Chunk           | The campaign finale. Stomps, charges, spits, burps, sheds.                       |
| Spitter  | Not built yet         | Attacks from range, so a closed door alone is not safe.                          |

## Technical notes

- **Drawing.** The world is drawn with Foldkit's `Canvas`, plus the upstream `Canvas.Image` through a patch (see `patches/README.md`). Every fixed sprite, glyph, and baked text is one image from a PNG data URL; shapes that change every frame stay rectangles. Scene code builds Groups with the `group` helper in `src/sprite.ts`, since `Canvas.Group` parses every nested shape through Schema on each call.
- **The level is data.** The Model's `level` names the level and its open exits, and `levelOf(model)` returns the built `Level` with its tiles, doors, and spawn doors. Every function that reads the map takes that `Level`, and caches key on it, since it is a new record whenever an exit opens. `openExit` opens one. The wave tables still name the parlour's four doors; the kitchen decides how spawns use the doors an exit adds.
- **The camera** is derived from the player, the level, and the window in `src/camera.ts`; only its aim lean lives in the Model. The scene canvas draws the world in one Group offset by the shake minus the camera; the overlay is screen space.
- **Depth.** Standing things are sorted by the y of their feet (`src/depth.ts`). Shadows and bullets sit under them; flashes, bars, and popups over them. The room is one image under everything, so the wall face around a doorway in a top wall is drawn again in the sort as an occluder at the floor line (`src/occluders.ts`): crops of the same image, the lintel and the wall beside the opening. Whatever is still in the doorway is behind it and its bar is hidden; whatever has stepped in is in front. Side and bottom doors need none, since their walls are drawn flat and a zombie in them already stands in front of the wall behind it.
- **Bigger maps.** A large manor may want the floor cut into cached tiles drawn only near the view, and the alive cap may need to count only zombies near the player. Measure frame cost again when zombies are drawn off screen.
- **Input.** Touch uses a document-level pointer Subscription that tracks each finger by `pointerId`. Frame ticks and pointer moves stay out of DevTools history.
- **Tests.** Game logic is covered by Story tests that replay Messages, and determinism is itself tested.

### Risks

- **Phone frame time.** Frame time is measured only in headless Chrome with CPU throttling, which slows JavaScript but not the GPU. A real mid-range phone has not been checked (waiting on the owner). `src/perf.scene.test.ts` budgets the shapes and paint operations of a worst-case room, and `bun run perf:check` gates frame time against `tools/audit/frame-baseline.json`. The method is in `tools/audit/README.md`.
- **What keeps it cheap.** Sprite images instead of rectangles (the worst room went from 2,656 shapes to 687 on 2026-10-28), the alive cap of 20 (at 4× throttling a room at the cap stays near 10 ms p95; 30 dropped frames at 6×), `isAnyOf` instead of `guards` in per-frame code, and a flow field rebuilt only when the player changes tile or the level changes.
- **Determinism and juice.** Animation, particles, and shake live in the Model and use the seeded generator. Sound and music play through Commands and never feed back into the game.

## Reference notes: Zombie Estate 2

From the fan-written [Zombie Estate Wiki](https://zombie-estate.fandom.com/wiki/Zombie_Estate_2), unchecked against the game. A record of what the original did, not a specification, and nothing here is decided. The names, items, and numbers are the original's; this game uses its own.

- **The game.** By Sad Spaghetti Entertainment, on Steam in 2017 after Xbox: online co-op waves, 35 characters, over 100 weapons, 30 stat-boosting hats, and 6 levels.
- **Levels.** Six maps (an estate, a high school, a mall, a skyscraper, a desert town, an office). The wiki's strategies agree: fight in open areas, small rooms are where a horde overwhelms you, and some maps spawn zombies inside rooms or corners.
- **The shop** is a character behind a sign somewhere on the map, locked during a wave, so its place is part of the level's design.
- **Modes.** Casual and Hard, up to 15 waves with up to 3 continues, and Unlimited with zombie health rising each wave.
- **Currencies.** Money that resets each game, and points earned at the end of a game that unlock characters and hats for good.
- **Ammo** comes in four kinds, each for certain weapons, bought in the shop and sometimes dropped.
- **Hats** each give a buff and some a debuff. **Traps** are not documented; the closest are deployables such as totems and a turret launcher.
- **What it suggests.** Walking to the shop ties it to the room choice (built). Small rooms should be risky. Maps of different shapes make the same guns play differently.
